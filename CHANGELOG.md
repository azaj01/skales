# **Changelog**

All notable changes to Skales will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),

and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## v12.9.30 - DeepDive

**A release that shows its work, under a licence of our own.**

DeepDive is named after what the coding window does now. Instead of a summary and a path in a sentence, a run puts what it actually produced on the table: the picture a tool took, under the call that took it; the thinking behind a step; the cards a lookup brought back; code finally treated like code. That habit runs through the whole release, from the transcript to the plugin shelf to the Business page, and it is the reason a hundred and forty five fixes are listed here one by one instead of as "various improvements".

Skales also ships under its own licence from here on. Private use, study and teaching stay free of charge, exactly as they were, and commercial use needed a licence under the previous terms too. What changes for somebody already using Skales is nothing at all. What changes on our side is that it is written down: the whole agreement, in the language you chose, in front of you once rather than folded away, and the release you already have keeps the terms it was supplied under.

DeepDive opens a new season, and the record starts here.

### Added

- **Skales Business is back.** The Business page returned to the sidebar, and it is the one place in Skales that talks about money: what a commercial licence covers, and how a company starts - an evaluation licence, free, the full scope, no seat limit, confirmed in writing with an end date. There is no price on the page, and there will not be one in a build again: the commercial terms are read from the same live endpoint as the figures, so setting them later is a change on the server rather than a release. Underneath is the golden ticket, and it is a form of four short steps that saves after the FIRST one - your address and whether this is for work or for yourself is all that is really needed, so closing the window at step three loses nothing. The two steps after it ask how big the team is and what you actually need, so the reply you get is about your situation instead of a brochure. Two tick boxes stand in that first step, and they do different jobs: agreeing that your details are stored so the enquiry can be answered is required and links to the privacy notice next to it, while the newsletter is separate, starts empty and is never a condition of anything. Joining is not a contract and not a payment; nothing is charged and no card is asked for anywhere on the page. The figures in the headline are read live when the page opens, every one of them rounded down with a plus so none of them can overstate, and if they cannot be fetched the page shows the last measured ones and says nothing about it.

- **There is a way off every list we keep.** An address on the waiting list or the newsletter had no way out but writing to us. Every mail now carries a link that takes the address off all of them at once, without a login and without a form to fill in, and a newsletter subscription only starts once the link in its confirmation mail has been clicked. Unsubscribing does not delete what you sent us - your enquiry is still an enquiry - it stops the writing; erasing everything is still the separate request it always was, and it now really does reach the waiting list, which it never did before.

- **Skales now asks you to accept its licence, once, and puts the whole agreement in front of you.** The terms travelled with the download but nobody was ever asked to agree to them. The first-run setup now has a licence screen right after the language question, and it shows the agreement itself rather than a summary of it: the full text in a plain scrollable field, white paper and small print the way an installer does it, with the version and date above it and Accept and Decline below. English and German each have their own complete text, and the app shows the one your language is set to; the other ten languages read the English text until their own translation ships. The text is read from the licence file that ships beside the app, so what you read is what the package carries, and the screen names that file and links the published copy at skales.app/licence. Accepting records which version you accepted and when; a machine that was already set up is asked the same question once, over the app, and nothing else opens until it is answered - the Iris window, the Skales Code window and the Flow presenter included. Declining does not close Skales by itself: the screen behind it says that nothing on your machine is deleted, names the folder your data is in, links the last release under the previous licence and names that licence, and closing the app is a second, deliberate press - somebody who refuses has to be able to read those lines first. It is never asked twice for the same version, and a new licence asks again. You can read it back any time under Settings > Advanced, above the third-party notices.

- **You can report an answer that is offensive or plainly wrong, without leaving Skales.** Right-click any answer and the menu that already holds Copy, Rewrite and Regenerate now also holds Report. It opens a small window: an optional line for what is wrong with it, a Report button, and one sentence above it saying exactly what will be sent - the answer text and the name of the model that wrote it, never a key and never another message from the conversation. Nothing new is drawn on a message nobody reports. A report written with no connection is no longer lost: it waits and goes out the next time Skales starts, and the confirmation says which of the two happened instead of thanking you for something that never arrived. The same holding area now covers the ordinary bug report, which until now was written to a file on your disk and never tried again.

- **Accepting the licence now means it was written down, and Skales says so when it was not.** A machine whose data folder cannot be written to accepted the licence, opened, and met the same question again at every start, with nothing on screen to explain it. The press is now answered honestly: either the acceptance is stored, or the screen names the reason it could not be. Accept also stays switched off for as long as the agreement itself could not be put on the screen, with a line saying what to do about it, so the button can never collect agreement to a text nobody was shown. Declining stays available throughout, and the message that appears when something goes wrong follows your theme instead of a fixed red.

- **Declining the licence shows you where you stand before anything closes.** Pressing Decline used to close the window on the spot, which meant the two things that matter at that moment went past unread: nothing on your machine is deleted, and here is the folder your data is in; and the last release under the previous licence is still available, with a link to it. Both are now on screen, together with the way back to the licence, and Skales closes on a second, deliberate press.

- **Nothing that talks to the model opens before the licence is answered.** The lock screen held the main window, and the desktop companion, the on-screen pointer overlay and the introduction story opened beside it regardless: a machine that never accepted could use Skales through any of the three. All three now wait for the same answer, and they come up the moment it is given, without a restart.

- **The licence screen now says when the text in front of you is the English one.** Two complete texts ship with Skales, English and German, and every other language is served the English one until its own translation is ready. Nothing on the screen said so, so a reader whose app was in Spanish, Turkish or Japanese met a wall of English between a Spanish, Turkish or Japanese heading and a Spanish, Turkish or Japanese Accept button, with nothing to explain it. The line under the text now names it: the binding full text is shown in English, and a translation into your language will follow. It is shown only where it is true - a German or English reader is looking at their own text and is told nothing of the sort.

- **Deleting a conversation is not final any more.** A chat you delete was gone the moment you clicked, and the cards, the plan and everything else that belonged to it went with it. A delete is now a move: the conversation goes to a trash under Settings > Security and can be put back for a month, with the list saying what was removed, when, and how long is left to change your mind. Putting one back brings the whole of it - the transcript and the helper cards that hung under the answers - and it is in History, in the search and in the export again, where it was. Clearing a selection in History goes there as ONE row, named by how many conversations are in it, so one press is undone by one press instead of eighty; it takes one slot, so it cannot push out what you deleted yesterday. The trash empties itself, so nothing you deleted quietly lives on for ever. Incognito chats are not kept, because the badge said they would not be. Deleting a conversation stops whatever it was doing first, whether you delete one or a whole selection, so nothing keeps working and keeps costing on something you threw away - and what is kept for you is the finished answer rather than half of one. A conversation you put back comes back as a conversation and not as a job: it is where it was, it is idle, and it waits for you.

- **A task says what it actually did.** A task that ran on its own left one sentence when it worked and one when it did not, and nothing in between: which tools it used, in what order, and which one failed were gone the moment the run ended, so "why did it not send the summary" had no answer. Each task now carries an account of its recent runs under itself - how the run ended, when it started, how many tools it used and how many of those failed, then the steps in order with the failed ones marked. A run the app was closed in the middle of still has its record. Deleting a task takes its account with it.

- **Iris shows the helpers a spoken turn sent out.** Ask the voice surface for something that several agents share out between them and the eye showed a single line, while the chat window, the Code window and the phone all drew a card with one chip per helper and what it was really doing. Iris now shows that same card under the spoken line - live while they work, and still readable when they are done.

- **Skales can now check whether a tool server it set up actually answers.** A reader wrote in: asked to wire up an MCP server, the agent pointed it at a folder it had guessed, checked neither the folder nor the connection, and reported the task complete and the connection working. The audit found the uncomfortable half of that - of every tool Skales has, not one could test an MCP connection. The handshake existed, behind the Test button in Settings, and nothing the agent could reach ran it. So no model, however good, could have checked; it could only guess or refuse. There are two tools now: one lists the servers this machine has and what each is doing, the other opens a real connection and comes back with the tool names - and says "needs a sign-in" as something different from "broken", because those send you to fix different things.

### Fixed

- **A placeholder cannot reach the screen by itself any more.** The sentence that names a licence change was one of two places where a line asked for a piece of text and never handed over the value it leaves a gap for, so twelve languages would have printed the gap instead - and the other was the FTP result in Lio, which put the file count in front of a sentence that already had room for it inside. Both say what they mean now, and the check that holds the twelve language files against each other reads the code as well: a line that asks for a sentence with a gap in it has to fill the gap.

- **The licence notice reaches the page it was written for.** An update that comes under the new agreement announces itself once and then waits four hours for the next check - and the page carrying the notice is reached by FOLLOWING that announcement, which is a navigation. By the time it drew, the announcement was gone, and the only thing left that still knew was the main process, which was not being asked about the licence at all. The notice was therefore missing on the one route anybody actually takes to it, leaving the Download button standing where the notice should have stood in front of it. The main process now answers that question too, the download's own progress no longer drops the answer, and the sentence names the version instead of printing a placeholder at the reader in twelve languages.

- **A link preview stops printing `&amp;` at you.** GitHub escapes its own description twice, so "Android & iOS" arrives as `Android &amp;amp; iOS`, and one pass of decoding left the second escape standing in the middle of the sentence. It is decoded to the end now, in both the chat and the coding window, without ever turning text a page escaped ON PURPOSE into markup - and four entities the reader had never heard of (`&#39;`, `&lt;`, `&gt;`, `&nbsp;`) are read too.

- **The weather line names the place that was found, not the words you typed.** The lookup has been forgiving for a while - it tolerates a misspelt country and a town the gazetteer has never heard of, and works its way outwards through the rest of the field until something matches - but the greeting printed the field back at you, so somebody who had typed "Aregentina" read their own typo beside a temperature that belonged to a place the app had correctly identified as being in Argentina. The line now says which place the temperature is for, and says the same thing on the second load as on the first. What it does when the field is EMPTY is untouched: no place is named at all, exactly as before. What it does when the field names a place nobody can find is untouched too, and deliberately - there the words you typed are the only honest thing to show. The assistant's own weather answer and the phone always named the found place; this was the one surface that did not.

- **The first participant in a Group Chat is readable in light mode.** Participants are told apart by colour, and the first one uses your accent - but its name tag on the opening screen, its letter badge in the setup and its whole message card were filled with the accent AND lettered in the accent, which is not low contrast, it is a solid block with the writing invisible inside it. The four participants below it, which draw from fixed blue, orange, purple and pink, were always right. The same pairing sat on the card that carries the closing verdict, and that card belongs to no participant, so it was unreadable in EVERY round rather than only in rounds that had a first participant. All four places now tint the fill and keep the accent for the writing, which is how the blue and orange participants beside them have always worked. Dark mode never had the fault and is untouched, line for line.

- **The mini chat window stops putting its header under the system's own buttons.** Every other window in the app leaves a strip clear at the top for the buttons the operating system draws over the page - the close/minimise/zoom lights on macOS, the caption buttons on Windows - and the compact window was the single one excluded from it, so on macOS the lights sat on the agent avatar and on Windows the caption buttons sat on the microphone, screen, + and expand controls. Two different corners, one missing reservation. It reserves the same strip as the full window now, from the same measurement, so the two cannot drift apart; on Linux, where the window keeps a real title bar and nothing is painted over the page, the strip is nothing and costs nothing. In the same place: the invisible band you drag the little window by was laid OVER the header rather than above it, and a drag band takes the click - so the top half of every button in that header quietly did nothing. The band IS the reserved strip now, and it stops short of the system buttons on either side, because a right-to-left Windows install puts them on the other one.

- **The figures under an answer are readable on a phone again.** Hovering the model line showed a box that ended mid-word, because it was drawn inside the message bubble and the bubble clips what leaves it. The breakdown and the bar's own figures now come from the one tooltip the app has had all along, which hangs off the document rather than inside the bubble and is placed against the line it explains: nothing is cut off at any window width, and it clears itself when the transcript scrolls instead of sticking after a tap. The numbers and the sentence a screen reader gets are unchanged.

- **An answer that writes an HTML tag keeps the words around it.** A tag in an answer was not shown and not spelled out either: it was dropped, and with it every character it covered - a sentence lost the piece the tag sat in, and a tag on a line of its own took every line under it off the screen until the model wrote a blank line or closed it. On a long answer that is most of the answer, arriving one visible line at a time. The tag is now shown as the characters the model typed, so nothing leaves the screen. Code blocks, comparisons like `a < b` and plain links are untouched.

- **A picture is read by the model you picked for the chat, even when the caption asks for work.** With the Advisor Strategy on, the picture turn was sorted by the caption alone - and a caption like "analyze this" or "find the error" reads as a task, so the planner answered instead of your chat model, which is exactly what that strategy was fixed not to do. A turn carrying a picture now goes to your chat model, whatever the caption says.

- **A picture no longer costs a capable model its tools.** Whether a picture turn keeps its tools was decided in the window, against the model shown there, and then held for the whole answer - while the model that actually answers is settled later and, with the Advisor Strategy on, is never the same one. So a model that can see the picture and act on it was disarmed by a verdict about a different model. The question is now asked of the model that answers, at the moment it is known, on every surface that takes pictures.

- **Joining through the Business page is now recognised on the Skales Plus page.** Both routes write `skalesplus_waitlist_email` into the settings file, but the Skales Plus screen only ever read the browser's local copy - so somebody who joined through Business was shown an empty form and asked for the same address a second time. It reads the same authority the Business page reads, keeps the local copy as the fallback when the settings file cannot be reached, and draws nothing decisive until it knows, rather than offering a join button and taking it away a tick later.

- **Attaching a picture no longer takes the tools away from an Ollama Cloud model.** A reader reported that the thinking and tool cards vanished from a chat the moment an image was in it. Half of what they saw is how it should be: a model that reads the picture itself answers in one go, so there is no vision step to show and, on a question that needs no thinking, no reasoning to fold open - ask something harder and the fold is back and stays. The other half was real. A model served from ollama.com through your local daemon - anything carrying a `:cloud` tag - is frontier-size and can call tools, but Skales judged it like a small local captioner whenever the daemon had said nothing about it, which is the ordinary case on an older daemon or on a model list fetched before the Ollama key was set, and sent the picture turn with the whole toolset stripped. The answer came back and nothing could be done with it. The tag decides now: a cloud model keeps its tools, a model your own hardware runs keeps the careful rule, and what the daemon itself reports still outranks both. A picture sent to the Telegram bot was a second version of the same mistake - there the toolset came off for every model, whatever it could do - and it now asks the one question the chat window asks.

- **Skales says what it checked, and says when it did not check.** The rule against claiming an unverified result was written into Skales seven times, once per tool, each after its own incident: a browser click that reports an unconfirmed publication, a desktop click that has to name what should be true afterwards, a video whose description never claims to have seen between two frames. Seven correct rules and no principle - so the case none of them covered, writing a file and calling the thing it configures finished, had nothing to say against it. The principle is now stated once, and it survives every level of prompt compression, because the shortest prompt goes to the models that need it most: a claim that something runs, is connected, is installed or exists at a path rests on a tool result from this run, or is handed over as unverified with the check named.

- **A configuration that names a place you do not have says so when it is written.** Writing a file always succeeds, which is exactly what made a guessed data folder look like a finished task. When Skales writes or edits a configuration file, the absolute paths inside it are now checked against the disk, and the ones that are not there are named on the result - the file was written, that is all that was verified. It is a fact, not a refusal: a config may legitimately name a folder that is about to be created. URLs, routes, globs and media types are not mistaken for paths, and a file that is not configuration is left alone.

- **The status report stops telling a subscriber they have no key.** Asked to audit itself, Skales read one field to decide whether the active provider was set up - the field a pasted key goes in, which a ChatGPT subscription leaves empty. So the app reported "no API key" for the very subscription that was answering the question. It asks the shared question now. In the same pass, the description of the MCP section was corrected: it claimed there was no per-server Test control, while that button has been in Settings all along.

- **Skales Visuals works on a ChatGPT subscription.** A signed-in subscription was told it had no API key - by a design tool, in the same session in which every chat answer came through that very subscription. A subscription keeps its credential in a different field from a pasted key, and there is one shared answer to "is this provider set up" that knows that; two places rebuilt the question by hand instead and dropped the half that knows about subscriptions. Both ask the shared one now. The other half of it: the design path only knew how to talk to pasted-key providers at all, so even a correct answer would only have moved the failure one step. It speaks the subscription's own protocol now, refreshes the sign-in before a long run rather than in the middle of one, and draws the page as it arrives instead of at the end. The same hand-written question sat in the plain-chat path, where it did something quieter and worse: it decided a subscriber had nothing configured and answered them on a different provider without saying so.

- **The effort dial works on the models your subscription actually has.** The dial read from a list of five model names written into Skales, while your account's own model list - which says exactly which effort levels each model accepts - was right there and consulted only by the half of the app that sends the request. So on six of seven subscription models the dial sat inert in the composer and could not even be set, while the wire would have carried the level. Every surface that draws the dial asks with the account's list now: chat, a new chat, Flow's home and workspace, and the Code window.

- **A turn that ends with nothing says why, on a ChatGPT subscription too.** Skales has a whole apparatus for an answer that comes back empty: it names the cause, retries once with a bigger output budget when hidden reasoning ate it, and tells you which two settings help. None of it ran for the subscription, because that one path filled the emptiness with the sentence "No response received" before anything could look - and a sentence is content, so everything downstream saw a normal answer. Three more paths did the same with "No response." Empty leaves as empty now, and the machinery that explains it runs everywhere.

- **"Take a screenshot" takes a screenshot again.** Desktop control has two ways of looking at your screen - photographing it, and reading the controls on it the way a screen reader does - and the second one was added to the toolbox without the first one beside it: the two halves ended up in different drawers, and the drawer the agent opened held only the reading half. So a plain "take a screenshot of my screen" got no picture. Instead the agent tried to read the screen five times over, hit the permission that reading needs, and reported back that the screenshot had failed for want of that permission - a permission a screenshot has never needed, while the tool that would have taken one sat in the drawer it never opened. Desktop control is one drawer now, and the agent is told where it is, so one reach brings both ways of looking. It is also told what it plainly was not: that photographing the screen and reading it are two different permissions on macOS, and that a wall in front of one says nothing about the other. Switching off the picture-reading model no longer reads as "no screenshots" either - taking one never needed a model.

- **A failing command is not tried five times in a row any more.** Screen and browser actions were exempt from the brake that stops an agent repeating itself, and for a good reason: looking at a screen twice gives two honest answers, because the screen moves. But the exemption covered the failures too, where nothing between two identical attempts can change the outcome - so a wall that could not be climbed was walked into five times, and a question that had already been answered was asked four times, before the run gave up on its own. Repeating something that worked is still free. Repeating something that failed, unchanged, is now stopped at the third try and the agent is told what failed and to try another way - and if it succeeds in between, it starts with a clean slate.

- **A screenshot Skales took is drawn as the tool's picture, and a second look at the same screen is still a second look.** The record that carries a tool's image to the model travels in a user-role message, because that is the only slot a provider wire has for a picture. The half that keeps a reader from mistaking it for something the person typed was working: no blue bubble, no internal fence text on screen, one icon under the picture, and an exported transcript with only the person's own words in it. The other half was not. Where each picture came from was assembled and then dropped on the way to the session file, so a stored image had no provenance at all; and a picture was identified by the path it happened to lie at, which meant the same capture shown again under a copied name counted as a second photograph. Both are fixed, and identity now follows the bytes, with the tool that produced the record deciding whether it claims to have made the picture or only to show it again - two screenshots of a screen that did not move are still two captures.

- **A Claude model can use a tool and carry on.** With Anthropic picked as the provider, the first step of a run went through and the next one came back as a refusal from the provider the moment a tool had actually run - so anything that needed a tool ended on an error instead of an answer, and starting several helpers at once looked like only the first one worked, because every helper after it inherited the finished work of the one before. Claude is now told what was called and what came back, everywhere Skales talks to it, so a run continues where it left off, several results of one step arrive together instead of one at a time, and a picture a tool produced is still seen. A call whose arguments arrived damaged is handed over as what arrived rather than as nothing, so the model can correct itself instead of repeating the same empty call, and a step that is refused says in the diagnostics that it was the step returning results - the one line that tells an outage apart from a model that stopped mid-answer.

- **The products keep their names in every language.** A Skales Visual was a "Skales görseli" in Turkish, a "Skales vizual" in Croatian, "Visuales de Skales" in Spanish and a transliteration in Japanese and Chinese; Swarm was an "Enjambre" and a "Sürü", Discover was "Explorar" and "Keşfet", Wrapped was an "Özet", and Teams, Workflow, Playground, Playbooks, Studio and Spaces were spelled out in kana, hangul, hanzi and Cyrillic. A name is not a word: a reader who sees something else cannot find the thing again in the sidebar, cannot search for it, and cannot ask about it by name. All nineteen coined names now read the same letters in all twelve languages, on a tab and in the middle of a sentence alike - and the words that really are words, Settings, Tasks, History, Memory, Browser, Chat, Dashboard and Autopilot among them, stay in the reader's own language, where they belong. Grammar is left alone: Croatian still says "u Discoveru", Turkish "Skales Local'i", Korean "Skales Visuals-ro", and German still writes "Skales-IQ-Daten" the way German writes a compound.

- **The English text was the source of it.** The /image and /video command lines asked for "a 1:1 Skales visual" in lower case, and eleven translators were told by that, correctly, that it was an ordinary noun - so eleven of them translated it. English now writes the name too, and the gate that holds the list reads the English file as well as the other eleven, so the next name can no longer be lost by being written small.

- **Skales Visuals now works the way it reads, and says so when it cannot.** Asking for a graphic in a chat could take five minutes and come back with "The model returned nothing", or with three words that named nothing at all, and Skales could not afterwards tell you whether anything had been made. Three things were true at once and all three are fixed. The model that designs was never the one you chose: it came from the code builder's own saved configuration, a screen whose field says "writes the code" and which nothing in the app connected to your graphics. Designing now follows the studio row under Settings > AI Providers > Per-Mode Model Overrides - a control that was already there and did nothing - and with the row untouched it follows your active provider, or your own machine when you set Skales Local as your main model. The prompt enhancer, the captions, the storyboard, the scene animator, the template prompt writer and a visual made with /image or /video all follow the same choice; the code builder keeps its three models for building software and decides nothing outside it.

- **A design that comes back empty now says why, and tries the one thing that helps.** A model that thinks before it writes can spend everything it is allowed to produce inside that thinking and hand back nothing, and running the same request again spends the same minutes the same way. The design is now asked again with more room to write, and if that changes nothing, once more with the thinking switched off - the same rescue the chat has had for months, now on the same wire. When nothing still comes back, the answer says the budget went into the thinking, roughly how much of it, and which kind of model gets past it, instead of saying the model returned nothing.

- **A visual that fails leaves a record.** A failed design wrote nothing anywhere, so asking Skales afterwards what went wrong was answered out of an empty log - and the emptiness was read as proof that nothing had happened, while the picture was sitting in the gallery. A failed attempt is now written down like a successful one, where the diagnostics you can read back already look, and it is never counted as a design you made.

- **A long design is no longer cut off by the clock it was measured against.** The chat's request for a graphic and the design itself were given exactly the same amount of time, so the request always gave up a moment before the design could explain itself, and what reached you was "fetch failed". The chat now asks for a graphic the same way Studio and Flow do - watching it as it is written rather than waiting in silence - and it is given more time than the design itself has, so the sentence the design had ready is the one you get. A failure that really is a connection fault now names what failed instead of hiding it.

- **A provider outage reported mid-answer is no longer read as an empty answer.** Some providers report that the model behind them is down by saying so inside an otherwise healthy response. Skales read past it, ended the turn with nothing, and said the model had returned nothing - so an outage at the other end looked like a fault on your machine. Both the chat and the design path now read it and say what the provider said.

- **Studio and Flow render video again.** Before encoding, Skales proves that the composition really paints the frame it is asked for: it seeks to eight frames, out of order, visits three of them twice and compares what it got. The comparison was made byte for byte, and a browser does not draw type byte for byte - measured on a finished eight-second film, 18 of 2,073,600 pixels came back one unit apart on one colour channel, from the way the letters of one headline were smoothed the second time. Invisible on screen, invisible in the file, and enough to refuse the render with "frame 239 changed when sought twice". The two captures are now compared as pictures: a frame that MOVED is still refused, and a frame the rasteriser rounded differently is not. A refusal also says what it saw - how many pixels differed, and by how much - instead of naming only the frame.

- **Reporting an answer now works in every language, not only in the ones written with Latin letters.** The report was measured in characters and Skales accepts it by size, so a flagged answer in Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Russian - or simply a German one with enough umlauts - was two to three times too large, was turned away, and was then retried five times with exactly the same content while the confirmation said it was on its way. Every report is now trimmed to a size that actually gets through, and it says in the text when it had to be shortened. An answer whose cut fell in the middle of an emoji was rejected outright for the same reason, on every language; the cut can no longer land there. The ordinary bug report travelled the same road and had grown too large in the same way - a long one now arrives instead of vanishing, and the crash details that used to be sent in a field nothing stored are written into the report itself.

- **A report that cannot be delivered says so, instead of promising it is on its way.** Skales could not tell "you are offline" from "Skales refused this report", so a report that would never be accepted was announced as waiting and quietly given up on days later. A refusal is now said plainly and at once, a report that really is waiting still goes out at the next start, and one that has run out of attempts is written into the log the Diagnostics card in Settings shows, so the promise and the outcome can no longer disagree. The report is also put into the waiting list BEFORE it is sent rather than after the send fails, so closing the window mid-send no longer loses it, and every report carries an identity of its own so a slow answer cannot turn one report into two.

- **A long spoken instruction survives a failed transcription.** The recording is kept under the conversation before anything is sent, so a provider error, a missing key, a reload or a restart leaves it recoverable with one button instead of asking for the whole paragraph again. Transient failures retry themselves with a growing delay; a missing key waits for the key. Retrying never sends the same words twice and never runs the same tool twice.

- **Iris no longer takes her own voice for your approval.** A spoken "yes" only counts when it came from a microphone that opened after she stopped talking, so a permission question coming back through the speakers cannot approve a tool. Saying no is still always heard, the buttons always work, and a refused answer says why instead of being swallowed.

- **The orbit shows the whole result, not the first part of it.** A long report, document or answer is paged inside the frame with a place to read on and a line saying how much is left, instead of being cut at a fixed length with nothing to indicate it. Rows and columns are drawn as a table, and every row of a list arrives. Closing the window and coming back rebuilds what was on screen from the conversation itself.

- **A video you cut keeps everything you set, all the way to the file.** Where each clip sits, its own sound, the crossfades between them, how loud the voice and the music are, the music ducking under the voice, the subtitles, the shape of the frame and whether it leaves as MP4 or WebM are all part of the saved project now, instead of being rebuilt from guesses on the way out. Export a second time and you get a second file, of exactly the cut you were looking at.

- **Code follows the rules your project already has, at the place they apply.** A repository often carries rule files at several levels - one at the top, another in a subfolder, another for one kind of file - and they were being applied loosely. Before anything is written, Skales now works out which of them apply to that exact path and in which order, whether the change comes from the main run, a helper agent or your phone. And a preview can be checked for more than how it looks: what the page logged, what it asked the network for, what failed to load, and a real picture of it. An app that needs its own address, a sign-in or its own stored data can be moved into the session's shared browser rather than an isolated one.

- **A request that is really a job is recognised in all twelve languages.** Asking for several steps of work in one sentence turns into a Goal at the same point whether you wrote it in English, German, Russian or Japanese, short or long. A plan you are only quoting or explicitly ruling out stays an ordinary message, something you want repeated stays a schedule rather than becoming a Goal, and whenever a message is promoted you are told in the conversation and the Goal keeps saying so afterwards.

- **You can edit a file yourself in Code, and the tree keeps up.** The review panel saves whole text files, with the keyboard where you expect it, and refuses to overwrite a file that changed on disk while you were typing - it tells you instead of silently winning. The file tree comes back with the project's own selection and folders still open, reloads only the folder that actually changed, shows a folder it could not read with a Retry rather than an empty space, and can be walked entirely from the keyboard.

- **A finished goal is checked against every requirement you actually stated.** A long brief no longer loses its must-haves after the seventh one. Where the proof is a file, the file's content is fingerprinted and checked again later - so a proof that was true an hour ago and has since been edited is caught - while something already done out in the world stays done and is never done a second time to re-prove it.

- **Lio tells an interrupted build apart from a finished one.** A build that was stopped, failed or only got halfway is recorded as that, worked out from the steps that really finished and the files that really have something in them. Picking it up again starts at the first unfinished step, and deploying or asking for changes stays locked until the build has actually proved itself.

- **A small change to a Lio project no longer costs a full rebuild.** Asking to change a word, a colour, a label or a bit of spacing reads just the part of the file it concerns and makes one precise edit, while a real structural change still goes through the full plan-and-review path. Each project keeps a record of what it was before and after, how many rounds it took, which files were read and changed, how long it took, and anything that changed that you did not ask for.

- **Falling back to another provider no longer does your work twice.** When a provider fails halfway through a turn and another takes over, Skales now knows which actions had actually gone through - a mail sent, a file written, something created out in the world - and does not repeat them. That memory survives a reload, so a resumed run does not send the message again either. A separate run you start yourself is untouched by it.

- **A picture sent from the phone is a real file on the computer again, and the answer says where.** An image attached on the phone lands in the same folder a picture dropped into the desktop composer lands in, so asking to edit, convert or upload "this image" finds something to open. The sentence naming the file comes from the same place the desktop's does, so a model that cannot see the picture is told that plainly instead of guessing at it.

- **Working from the phone now runs the same way as working at the computer.** A request from a paired phone enters exactly the machinery a desktop request enters, gets a confirmation that it was really accepted, and is then followed as the one true run - through queueing, an approval card, Stop, losing connection and restarting. The transcript comes back whole rather than cut at a fixed length, and asking for several agents no longer quietly puts the work on a second, different track.

- **The browser you can see and the browser Skales drives are the same browser.** The panel and the automatic tools now agree at every moment on which page is open and which page an instruction refers to. Switching between them does not leave an instruction pointing at a page that is gone, reloading rejoins the page that is actually there, the panel follows along when Skales navigates, and two runs that both want the browser are told which one has it instead of silently pulling the page out from under each other.

- **Helper agents get the same senses and the same off switch as the run that asked for them.** A helper started inside a turn inherits that conversation, pictures included, and Stop reaches it and its tools rather than only the parent. The planning and reviewing roles in Code can read files and run checks through the same guarded set the writing role uses. And browser actions keep the run that opened the page as its owner: a competing request from a chat, a coding session, a playbook or a plugin is visibly refused instead of quietly taking the page over.

- **Memory recall only brings back something that actually matches.** Its own bookkeeping - scan state, lists of deletions, a malformed record - could crowd out the whole recall window; it no longer counts as a memory. Recency now only decides the order of things that are relevant, instead of pushing an unrelated fresh fact into your next answer. And short names and words stay findable in all twelve product languages, both when they are stored and when they are looked up.

- **Forgetting a conversation forgets what was learned from it.** Deleting a session also removes the memory built out of it, including any day summary it fed, and leaves a marker so a later background pass cannot quietly reconstruct the record you deleted.

- **Pushing a day plan to the calendar cannot duplicate it.** If the push is retried - a hiccup, a lost answer, a resumed run - the events that already exist are reused rather than created again, an event that was made but never confirmed is found, a block you edited is updated, and a half-finished push carries on from where it stopped.

- **Code keeps what you had half-done with the session it belongs to.** The text you were typing, the files you attached, the skills you picked and how hard the model was set to think all follow the session you opened, across switching sessions and across a reload. A first send that fails keeps the turn instead of eating it, attaching more than fits is named rather than silently dropped, and a team run receives the same files, pictures and skills a single agent would have received.

- **A command you started in Code keeps running while you are not looking at it.** Hiding the panel, reloading, or going off to another project no longer kills it; reopening the panel shows you everything it wrote in the meantime. Closing that tab still ends it, and quitting Skales still ends what is left.

- **A Flow video is proved before it is encoded.** The renderer waits until the motion engine is really ready, steps forwards and backwards through the difficult frames, and refuses a frame that is missing, broken or different every time it is asked for - naming the frame. A file that was encoded and a file that passed its checks on length, size, frame rate and colour are now reported as two different things, so a render that failed its checks can no longer be handed to you as finished.

- **A Flow render or review survives closing the project.** The work is recorded against the project, the exact version of the source it came from and the stage it had reached, so reopening picks it back up - rechecking if the source changed since, continuing a correction that was already in progress, and only accepting that a correction worked after seeing that it changed the very thing it was meant to change.

- **A proposed day plan is checked before it replaces the one you have.** Blocks have to be well formed, sit at real times in your own time zone, last longer than nothing on the same day, fall inside your working hours and not collide with your calendar, your fixed blocks or each other. A malformed answer or a provider failure stays visible as what it is and leaves the plan you already had alone.

- **Stop stops the helpers too, at the same moment.** Pressing Stop reaches every helper agent's model request and every tool it is running, so nothing new starts afterwards and no helper quietly begins another paid round. Helpers running side by side also spend from the one conversation budget: calls already in flight may finish, and then they all stop before the next model call once their combined spend reaches the ceiling you set.

- **What you review in Code is what gets committed.** A commit preview shows the exact state you staged and refuses to go ahead if that state changed while you were reading it; an action on part of a change is refused if the change has moved on; and undoing a checkpoint keeps later edits and shows you the conflict instead of overwriting them. Undoing a whole turn is worked out in full first, so repeated or multi-file edits either all come back or nothing is touched.

- **Work done by a helper agent in its own copy of the project is never lost in the handover.** What it changed is measured from where its copy started, so work it already committed counts too. The parent confirms the handover arrived before cleaning anything up - and if the handover is refused, the helper's result fails loudly and its work is kept for you to recover rather than swept away.

- **Chat shows the local context window without a trip to Settings.** The meter and its detail read from the local runtime that is actually serving, keep what they say tied to the endpoint and model you selected, and stop an older reading from reinstating a stale window after you switch models.

- **Stopping the voice keeps it stopped while a fallback is still loading.** A reply that arrives late can no longer start the old voice over the next thing you play, and a failure to speak keeps its real reason instead of a generic one.

- **A second local turn no longer refuses to use the model already loaded.** The check before a run now compares the memory the model needs with the memory the machine has, so the memory the loaded model itself occupies no longer looks like a reason to say no.

- **A closed connection no longer takes the local server down with it.** Someone closing a page while a local answer is still being written is recognised as exactly that and survived. Every other write failure still goes down the normal error path, and the diagnostics keep the real reason.

- **Local hardware diagnostics keep the whole device name.** The server's output is read across however many pieces it arrives in, so a detected graphics card and how much of the model it is carrying survive startup and repeated loads instead of arriving cut in half.

- **A local context window follows the model it belongs to.** The window a model is really being served with is carried for models that are idle as well as loaded, refreshed when the Local panel looks, and kept separate per endpoint - so the same model served from two places no longer shares one stale number.

  Flow's landing check, project model choice and shared surface routing now use

  as completed: an action still waiting for your approval, a run that used up

Rotating the key in AI Providers then left the reader quietly using the old one until every picture came back as a failed request. All four are open again and none of them wider than before - the internet only to the sites the skill's permissions name, the two folders only because they were handed over, and the settings with anything that looks like a secret removed, exactly as a command run from a shell gets them. ### Changed

- **The licence question is asked before the update is delivered, not after it.** Automatic downloads are on by default, so a version that comes under a new agreement used to arrive downloaded and installed, and the licence screen came afterwards - the decision landed after the delivery. An update that crosses the licence boundary now holds: the notice names the change, links the text, and the Download button is the decision. An installation that already runs on this agreement updates exactly as before, and somebody who presses Download themselves is never held up. The one group this cannot reach is the one already on the old licence, because the holding happens in the build that does the holding - so the release feed itself now carries the notice, which is the only thing an older Skales shows you that we write at release time.

- **Two lines stand between the licence and the buttons.** Private use stays free of charge, as before; commercial use needs a licence, and that was true under the previous licence too. They sit below the text rather than above it, because they are the last thing read before the press. There is nothing to click that classifies you as private or commercial: a press cannot make that true, and a promise that "nothing changes for you" would be a promise to a group the app cannot identify.

- **Declining says what you keep, and stops handing out an old build.** The refusal screen shows the data folder, untouched, and now says the thing that is actually true and costs nothing: an installation that was already running is unaffected and stays on the licence it was supplied under. It names that licence - the Business Source Licence 1.1 - and quotes the part of it that has not changed: its Additional Use Grant covered personal use, learning and non-commercial internal testing, so commercial use needed a licence there too. What it no longer does is offer a download of a superseded release. The same screen is shown to somebody installing for the first time, a superseded build gets no security fixes, and an installed copy can never change the address it shipped with - so the one link goes to the published licence and nowhere else.

- **The app carries no price, and it never will again.** The Business page held twelve euros a seat in twelve languages, in a block where the numeral and the unit were two type roles - and a website can be corrected every day while an installed copy keeps what it shipped with until somebody updates. In its place stands what is true today: an evaluation licence, free, the full scope, no seat limit, confirmed in writing with an end date, which is a written commercial licence at no charge rather than a stretched trial. The commercial terms come down the same live wire as the figures, so setting a price later is a change on the server and not a release, and a machine with no connection shows the honest statement instead of a number that has aged.

- **The Business page stops saying things it is not allowed to say.** "Free, with your own key" now names which half is free where it is said: your own key decides who pays the model, the licence decides whether you may use Skales commercially. The line about nothing being locked in no longer says "stop paying and the free agent keeps working with your own key" - a sentence about the end of a payment, with the licence unmentioned, reads on a business page as a free commercial alternative. And the waiting list says at the point of consent that Skales may write about commercial licensing, with what the box asked for stored beside the privacy notice version it was agreed against.

- **What the offer carries instead of a badge: your logo on our page, and your name to your own clients.** The fifth line of the evaluation licence used to promise a founding badge on an account and thirty minutes on the phone. From v13 it is a company logo under "Trusted by" on skales.app, linked back to your own site, and your name listed there as a qualified contact for setting up your clients - a link somebody can follow and a mention somebody can act on, instead of a mark inside an account only you can see.

- **The v13 section says what is coming, not what is missing.** The band over it read "not built yet", and the paragraph under it listed what the download you can get today does NOT contain. That it comes with v13 already says it is not here now. Nobody reading a business page should have to learn our release plan to understand an offer.

- **FFmpeg is in the third-party notices, with its licence and where to get its source.** Skales uses it to merge audio and render video, and it ships in the package - but only the small JavaScript wrapper around it was ever attributed. The build that ships carries the GPL, version 3 or later; FFmpeg runs as its own program and is never built into Skales, so those terms cover FFmpeg and not the application around it. Its full licence text and FFmpeg's own notice now travel with every download, and the notice says where the matching source can be had.

- **Everything in the download is attributed, and a new piece cannot slip in unattributed.** The notices used to be assembled from a list that could not see the programs added to a package last. Whatever ends up in a download now has to be named and described before the build will finish.

- **The third-party notices read as the public document they are.** The file ships with every download and is published beside the licence, and it was carrying a block of internal notes and a handful of internal file paths out with it. Both are gone, and it stays that way.

- **A picture a tool took is shown in the coding window, under the call that took it.** Until now a screenshot taken during a run reached Skales Code as a message from YOU: pictures travel to a model in a user-role slot, because that is the only slot every provider wire has for an image, and the window read that transport detail as authorship. So a live check with two screenshots in it produced a turn on the right-hand side, in your name, carrying the fence written for the model - with an edit button and a send-again button on it. The chat has told the two apart for months; the window now asks the same reader, and goes one better than the chat can: it has a line per step, so each picture hangs under the call that produced it rather than under the round. A tool that merely shows a picture again does not draw it a second time - it says it showed one from further up - while two captures of a screen that did not move stay two looks at the screen, however identical their bytes.

The same thumbnails the composer showed you before you sent are in the log afterwards, and after a restart, and any of them opens full size.

A page that never thought about its edge gets one, and takes it back with one line of its own.

- **A plugin you start by hand opens on the house look, in your language.** The blank page it creates carries the same floor every bundled plugin does and says what it is in the language the app is set to, rather than in English regardless.

- **The Business page now shows what Skales already does instead of claiming what it might.** The page opens on one night's work - receipts read out of the mailbox into a month sheet, an invoice chased with the reminder already written, a post published with its image and its SEO, a summary sent to the phone - with the tool behind every line named. It says out loud that the reminder was drafted and not sent, because a scheduled run has nobody at the screen and sending waits for a click. The old opener promised an agent that "finds clients", and there is no lead, no source and no duplicate check anywhere in the tree to make that true.

- **Six things Skales already stands for, before anything about a price.** It runs on your machine; your key or no key at all; Skales IQ carries zero data retention on the request itself rather than as an account setting; it asks before it sends, publishes, deletes or spends; nothing locks you in, since a plugin exports as one file and the free agent keeps working with your own key; and erasure is a branch that runs, with a 36-month sweep behind it. Underneath them, the boundary in one line: this is not a certificate, and what a procurement reviewer gets is a document naming the providers a request can reach.

- **Skales Business is on the page, and marked as not built.** Workspaces, cost control with a usage journal and budgets that reserve before a run starts, infrastructure profiles for a company gateway, department packs, joining by invitation, SSO and SCIM, and an admin portal that runs without us. It is drawn as a rail with hollow marks rather than a row of cards with ticks, because a tick is a claim that something is there.

- **The free half is named before the paid half.** Ten plugins, every tool, triggers on the clock, a folder and the mailbox, and any provider key stand in their own column beside what the seat adds. A buyer does that subtraction anyway.

- **The offer is an object rather than a sentence in gold.** What it is stands at the display step with its scope beneath, and the five conditions run down the same object as a hairline column instead of five full-width cards that outweighed the offer itself. The geometry does not care whether the left cell holds a name or a figure, which is what lets the terms arrive over the wire. The partner terms and the v13 sunset are two statements on two lines, not one that trails off.

- **Enterprise and white label read as an invitation:** "Tell us what you need and we will talk about it. Starting in 2027."

- **Your memory has a ceiling now, and the Memory page says what it is.** The three record folders behind the counters grew by one file per turn and nothing ever trimmed them: on this machine 519 recent notes, 556 long-term records and 598 journal entries in eight weeks, of which 465 were written overnight by the dreaming pass rather than by anybody. That is not a disk problem, it is a reading one - the list is read from start to finish every time Skales builds a prompt, and at thirty-six thousand files that is a sixty-five millisecond pause on every single turn. Each folder now keeps its newest thousand records. Nothing you can still reach is lost by that: the prompt reads the newest five, the Memory page shows thirty, and the search reaches four hundred, so long-term had quietly passed the point of being findable months ago. What was filed on purpose is never rotated out, however old it is: a fact you asked Skales to remember, a todo or calendar note you gave the pointer, a pinned record, and the closed records that let a fact show its history. In the long-term store only the overnight pass is ever trimmed. One line under the three counters says all of this on the page itself, and Skales knows it too, so asking whether it keeps everything for ever gets an honest answer.

- **Building a prompt stopped re-reading the memory folder every time.** The list of recent notes was read fresh off the disk on every turn, and twice on a turn where the profile is withheld - once for the turn, once for the rebuild. It now reads through the same short-lived cache its sibling has used all along.

- **The sidebar opens with four entries you cannot lose, and five you can.** Dashboard, Chat, Code and Studio are the floor of the column now. Teams and Iris Orbit used to stand there too and could not be taken away, which is the wrong rank for a messenger a lot of people never open and a voice surface behind its own switch - both are ordinary rows now, and both can be sent into More like anything else. Nothing moves on a machine that is already set up: whatever your column looked like, it looks the same after the update, in the same order. A fresh install opens with Voice, Memory, Discover, Business and Skales Mobile under the four, and takes any of them away with one press. Custom Skills and Plugins are no longer handed out at the start - both are shelves for things you have built, both are empty on a new machine, and a row that opens onto nothing is how you learn that the rows are noise. They are one tap away under More, and whoever already had them keeps them.

- **Templates moved out of the column and into the System button.** The library of starting points and the Guide answer one question between them - what can I start from, and what can this machine do - and they stood a whole column apart. Templates is now a row in the same popup, directly above the Guide, and it still follows its own switch on the Add-Ons page.

- **Pointing Skales Code at a repository no longer throws you into a session.** Picking a folder with nothing typed opened a session on the spot, which took you off the landing before you had chosen a model, attached a file or picked an agent - off the one page whose whole job is making those choices. Picking now binds the folder and leaves you where you are: the chip names it, the branch appears beside it, and the @-menu lists the repository's files, all before a session exists. A press on the x lets the folder go again. The way in is the send button, or the "Open the session" button that appears beside the folder for the times you want the file tree and the terminal without typing anything first. Opening a folder from outside - `skales .`, a folder dropped on the dock, the folder's own context menu - still opens a session straight away, because that is a different request.

- **A session started from the Skales Code landing keeps what you set there.** The model you picked, the effort you dialled, the mode, the agent, the extensions, the skills you attached and the files you dropped were all read from a stale copy the moment the session opened: the model was never pinned, so the composer went on naming it while the run used the settings default; the effort quietly fell back to medium; and anything staged without a message was cleared. All of it is now written onto the session before it opens, and read back from there - so the phone opening the same session finds the same composer. If the model could not be pinned for any reason, it is no longer shown as pinned either.

- **Every question Skales asks before doing something now looks and reads the same.** The permission prompt, the offer to install a language checker, the offer to import another editor's MCP servers, the chat's approval card and Flow's all asked the same four things - allow this once, allow it for this session, allow it always, refuse - in five different shapes, and in three different orders: the refusal was last in the Code window, on the right in the chat and on the LEFT on the phone. They are now one card with one order everywhere: one answer per row, full width, the narrowest yes first and the refusal last and set apart, and each answer carries a plain sentence inside it saying how far it reaches and for how long. The language-checker card, which used to offer four bare words, says it too.

- **The Skales Code window's title bar no longer breaks apart on a narrow window.** A session title runs to sixty characters and the bar is a fixed 44 pixels that does not wrap, so on a phone-width or portrait-tablet window the title spilled out of the chrome. It is now cut with an ellipsis at every width; below 720 pixels the repository and branch step aside, and below 560 the title does too - both are still on screen, in the status bar at the foot and in the history column.

- **Wrapped is opened from Discover, not from the sidebar.** The weekly card draws its week from the feed and its Sparks, and a row of its own in the Main list was a door standing away from the thing it belongs to. The row is gone; a Wrapped button stands in the Discover header beside Spark, follows the add-on switch like the row did, and the palette still finds the page.

- **The chat sidebar's More block holds History and the Cockpit, and both can be pinned.** Group Chat, Browser, Playbooks and Add-Ons stood there a second time, opening as windows over the app, while the same four are rows of the Home column that open as pages; two doors with two behaviours for one place. The block now holds the two surfaces you reach for between conversations rather than during one - History searches what was said, the Cockpit shows the work that came out of it - and either can be pinned into the column, where it stands under Agents. The four others are reached where they live. Group Chat carries speech bubbles instead of a second pair of shoulders, so it is told apart from Teams at a glance.

- **A More row with nothing behind it is not drawn.** Pin every row out of the block, in either column, and More stayed - a chevron that opened an empty space and said nothing about anything. Both sidebars now leave the row out while it hides nothing, and bring it back the moment something is unpinned or a plugin, a widget or the DevKit gives it something to hold again.

- **Playbooks has no menu entry any more.** A playbook is recorded and replayed where the browsing happens - from the chat and inside the Browser surface - so a row in the Tools list was a door to a workbench nobody navigates to. The row is gone. Nothing else is: the page answers at /playbooks, the palette finds it, the add-on switch is where it was, and run_playbook and list_playbooks work from any chat.

- **The sidebar row that opens Iris Orbit is called Voice.** The row read "Iris Orbit" in every language, a product name where every other row says what it does; a person looking for the place to talk to Skales did not find the word. The row is now Voice, in all twelve languages, and the window it opens keeps its name.

- **Organization is opened from the chat, not from a menu.** A team of your own agents working one job is something you put to work from the conversation, and its row in the Tools list and in the chat column's More block was two doors to a place the chat already reaches. Both rows are gone; typing /organization opens it, the command stands in the slash menu with the others, and the palette still finds the page. An add-on that is switched off is not opened blind: the command names the switch instead. The Add-Ons card says where the door is now rather than marking the surface as parked.

- **The line under the wordmark at start changes.** The splash screen carried one fixed line. It now draws one of twelve at every start, from a list the phone and the computer share byte for byte, and the fixed line stays as the fallback when the list cannot be read.

- **The licence names its licensor at the end, not in its first lines.** The agreement opened with a full postal address, telephone number and e-mail address in its fourth line, before it had said a single word about the software. The address, the telephone number and the e-mail address now stand together in a section of their own at the end, section 12, where a reader who needs to write to us finds them; the first lines say who the licensor is and point there. The English and the German text changed in the same way, and nothing else in the agreement moved.

- **The licence screen says "du" in every language it speaks.** Skales addresses one reader, informally, and English and German did - but the licence screen changed to the formal voice the moment it was read in Spanish, French, Croatian, Turkish, Russian or Chinese, so the one screen everybody has to read was the one screen that spoke like a form letter. All twelve languages now use the same voice there: pronouns, verbs and imperatives together, not half a sentence at a time. The computer and the phone also say each of those sentences with the same words again - the line explaining that the binding text is in English had drifted into two versions, formal on one machine and informal on the other. The agreement itself is untouched and keeps its legal register, which is a different thing from how a screen talks to you.

- **A chat that is working keeps saying so, all the way to the last step.** The conversation decided whether anything had changed by looking at a handful of facts about the last stored message - how many there were, whose it was, how long it was, when it arrived. Everything a running turn knows that is not in that sample moved without the screen noticing: which step it is on, which tool is open, what its helpers are doing, what the browser is looking at. Measured over one ordinary turn with tools, seven polls out of thirteen carried a change nobody looked at - which is how an answer from an earlier turn could end up standing over a picture you had just re-sent, with no thinking panel under it. The run now carries its own version number, and the chat redraws when that number moves and at no other time; it also notices when something else has replaced what is on screen and puts its own view back. The working indicator hangs on the run as well: it used to leave at the first token and never return, so a turn with eleven more steps to go looked finished for all of them. It now steps aside while an answer is being typed out and comes back for every further step, and a thinking panel you opened stays open across the whole turn. The Skales Code window asks the same question of the same field, so the two windows cannot disagree about the same piece of work.

- **Skales Desktop now runs on a newer generation of its web engine.** The
  ground the app stands on was a whole generation behind, which meant every
  release carried weaknesses that are no longer repaired at the source. Moving
  up puts the app back on a foundation that is still maintained and still gets
  security fixes, and it changes how pages and requests are handed around
  inside the app. What you see and what you click stays exactly where it was:
  every surface, from Chat and Code to Studio, Flow, Iris and Settings, was
  re-checked against the new foundation before this release.

- **A picture the agent goes back to now says it is the same picture.** A run
  that takes a screenshot and later puts it back on screen drew it twice, with
  nothing to tell the two apart, so a long run read as if the agent had looked
  again when it had only shown you what it already had. The second one keeps
  its picture, because taking it away to save a few lines would hide that the
  agent went back to it, and carries a small "Shown again" chip beside the one
  that says it came from a tool. Two real looks at a screen that did not move
  are still two looks, and are never labelled that way, however identical they
  are.

### Removed

- **The install count, the monthly-active count and the star count.** The install figures came from the public aggregate's `installs_lifetime` and `installs_30d`, which are the count of DISTINCT IP ADDRESSES that asked the update endpoint for a version - the same source reports 639 devices active in thirty days. A figure nobody can explain when asked what it measures does not belong on a page that asks a company to sign something. What is left is countable: tools from the generated manifest, providers and models from the aggregate, countries, and languages from the locale files.

- **The Revenue Dashboard tile and the "lead scoring" claim.** Neither exists anywhere in the tree. The usage dashboard arrives with Skales Business and is listed there instead.

- **"A growing library of business plugins - included."** It promised plugins that do not exist, in the row of things you get. The five that do ship - Open Invoices, Receipt Inbox, Notice Radar, Meeting Prep, Page Watch - have a section of their own now, on the free side of the line where they belong.

- **"...and nothing to bill"** from the Skales IQ line. Beside something a company pays for it read as "free", and it was not true.

- **"WHAT PEOPLE ARE BUILDING".** Six unsourced claims about customers, under a heading that presented them as real.
